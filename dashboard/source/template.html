Who stays reliable when the game is on the line

Pressure on a ball is how much the result can swing on it, plus how far behind the side is and the stresses of the moment: a climbing required rate, a run of dots, walking in after a collapse.

The chart is a real tied T20I, ball by ball. The top line is the chance that the side batting first wins, from our Markov model of the innings. The bottom line is the batter's pressure score (SPI, 0 to 10). Hover or tap to read any ball.

How the pressure score is built

Every ball gets a score from 0 to 10. The biggest ingredient is leverage, the expected swing in win probability on that ball. The rest describes the situation the player is in. Pick a view:

    SPI = 10 × Σ weight × component × (0.5 + 0.5 × game alive)

    "Game alive" is 1 while either side has at least a 10% chance and falls toward 0 once the game is decided, so a lost cause counts for half.

    How players are scored

    Each ball is compared with what an average player would do in the same situation. Runs above expectation count for the batter; a dismissal costs whatever a wicket is worth at that moment of the innings. Estimates adjust for the quality of the opposition and are pulled toward the average when the sample is small.

    Evidence

    How many high-pressure balls the rating rests on.

    The data shows real differences in ability between players, but no measurable extra "clutch" skill on high-pressure balls beyond that ability. So a player's pressure level is his all-round ability, with high-pressure balls weighted more, plus a small, heavily shrunk adjustment for how he did under pressure.

    Notes

    • International status. Capped status and country come from T20I appearances in the data (2022–2026). Internationals capped only in other formats or before 2022 were corrected by hand and are marked "status corrected by hand" in their profile. Players with no international appearance are listed as uncapped Indian domestic players. Check current status before the auction.
    • Batting position is the player's median batting position across his innings. Bowling phase tags mark phases where a bowler sends down a large share of his balls (powerplay ≥ 30%, middle overs ≥ 45%, death ≥ 25%). His main phase is the one he bowls most relative to the league average.
    • Pressure level is runs above expectation per 100 high-pressure balls (bowlers: runs saved, with wickets valued). The bar shows a 90% interval; wide bars mean little evidence. P(top 10) is how often the player stayed in the top 10 when matches were resampled 60 times, shown for the top 30.
    • Phase and situation levels (for example "Pressure level, death") use only that slice of high-pressure balls and are shown when the player has at least 18–20 such balls.
    • Win probability comes from a Markov model of every delivery, solved exactly for each format and calibrated on held-out matches.
